\# ORIX — Brand Identity \& Logo Development Plan



\*\*Project:\*\* ORIX  

\*\*Category:\*\* Windows Desktop Interaction Layer  

\*\*Deliverable:\*\* Complete Brand Identity \& Logo System  

\*\*Brand Positioning:\*\* A new layer of desktop interaction.  

\*\*Primary Tagline:\*\* Your desktop, within reach.



\---



\## 1. Objective



Develop a distinctive, scalable, and recognizable visual identity for ORIX.



ORIX is a desktop interaction platform for Windows that introduces a persistent, contextual interface for accessibility, productivity, system controls, notifications, widgets, and personalized interactions.



The brand identity must communicate:



\- Presence — an interface that is always accessible.

\- Interaction — a new layer between the user and the operating system.

\- Connectivity — different modules working together.

\- Personalization — an adaptable and expressive experience.

\- Simplicity — sophisticated functionality without visual complexity.



The objective is to establish ORIX as an independent technology brand, not a visual imitation of macOS or an ordinary Windows customization utility.



\---



\## 2. Core Creative Direction



\### Design Philosophy



\*\*Minimal. Geometric. Futuristic. Precise. Timeless.\*\*



The identity should combine:



\- Premium desktop software aesthetics.

\- Modern geometric design.

\- Intelligent use of negative space.

\- Minimalist visual communication.

\- Distinctive, custom-designed typography.

\- A recognizable standalone symbol.



Avoid generic AI startup aesthetics, overly complex illustrations, excessive gradients, and literal representations of computer hardware.



The identity should remain recognizable without requiring the ORIX wordmark.



\---



\## 3. Primary Logo Concept — The Aperture



<badge>Preferred creative direction</badge>



Explore a custom geometric symbol inspired by an aperture, portal, and the relationship between O and X.



\### Conceptual meaning



\*\*O — Presence\*\*

\- An always-available interaction surface.

\- A portal into the desktop experience.

\- Continuity and accessibility.



\*\*X — Interaction\*\*

\- Convergence of different functions.

\- Connection between modules.

\- User-system interaction.

\- A central point of activity.



\*\*Negative Space — The Opening\*\*

\- Represents access to another interaction layer.

\- Creates visual distinction.

\- Makes the symbol recognizable without being literal.



\### Design requirements



\- Develop an original geometric construction.

\- Explore interrupted circles, rounded squares, and custom apertures.

\- Experiment with an O-shaped boundary and X-inspired internal geometry.

\- Use negative space intelligently.

\- Maintain visual balance and symmetry where appropriate.

\- Ensure the symbol works at very small sizes.



The final mark must not simply be a generic O with an X placed inside it. It should feel like one unified, custom-designed symbol.



\---



\## 4. Alternative Logo Explorations



Explore three distinct directions before finalizing the primary identity.



\### Concept A — The Aperture (O/X Monogram)



The preferred direction.



A custom geometric mark combining an interrupted O with an X-inspired internal structure.



\*\*Brand message:\*\* A new point of interaction.



\### Concept B — The Edge



A rounded geometric boundary with a distinctive cutout.



Inspired by the idea of ORIX living at the edge of the desktop.



\*\*Brand message:\*\* Everything within reach.



\### Concept C — The Nexus



A combination of interconnected geometric elements.



Represents a modular ecosystem where different desktop capabilities operate together.



\*\*Brand message:\*\* One layer. Infinite interactions.



\### Exploration requirements



For each direction:



\- Generate multiple genuinely different concepts.

\- Explore different silhouettes and proportions.

\- Test positive and negative space.

\- Compare geometric and organic interpretations.

\- Evaluate recognizability at small sizes.

\- Avoid making minor variations of the same logo and presenting them as separate concepts.



Select one direction based on originality, scalability, brand relevance, and visual recognition.

\## 6. Wordmark \& Typography



Develop a custom ORIX wordmark rather than simply using a default font.



\### Typography references



Explore:



\- Geist

\- Manrope

\- Inter

\- Sora



\### Customization opportunities



Pay particular attention to:



\- The geometry of O.

\- The leg structure of R.

\- The proportions of I.

\- The intersection and terminal geometry of X.

\- Letter spacing.

\- Overall wordmark balance.



The wordmark should feel technical and futuristic without becoming difficult to read.



Explore uppercase ORIX as the primary treatment.



Also evaluate a lowercase `orix` treatment as an alternative brand expression.

\## 7. Color System



The primary identity should remain monochromatic.



Mascots, interface elements, and feature-specific visuals can introduce additional colors.



\### Primary palette



| Color | Hex | Usage |

|---|---|---|

| Obsidian | `#111318` | Primary dark identity |

| Cloud | `#F4F5F7` | Primary light identity |

| Slate | `#858B98` | Secondary elements |

| Ice | `#A4C8FF` | Subtle brand accent |



\### Optional accent palette



| Color | Hex | Usage |

|---|---|---|

| Violet | `#8C72F5` | Expressive elements |

| Mint | `#50C8BC` | Positive interactions |

| Coral | `#FF985F` | Notifications and emphasis |

| Blue | `#79A8FF` | Interactive states |



\### Color principles



\- The primary logo must work in pure black and white.

\- Avoid relying on gradients for logo recognition.

\- Keep accent colors controlled.

\- Ensure sufficient contrast.

\- Support both dark and light environments.

\- Preserve the symbol's identity across all color treatments.



\---



\## 8. App Icon Development



The ORIX application icon is a critical brand asset.



Develop an icon based on the finalized standalone symbol.



\### Requirements



\- Recognizable at 16×16, 24×24, 32×32, 48×48, and 256×256.

\- Strong silhouette.

\- Clear negative space.

\- Balanced proportions.

\- Consistent rounded geometry.

\- Suitable for Windows icon formats.

\- Works on both light and dark desktop backgrounds.



Explore:

\- Flat monochrome treatment.

\- Subtle dimensional treatment.

\- Premium material-inspired treatment.



The primary icon should prioritize simplicity and recognizability over decorative detail.



\---



\## 9. Mascot Brand Relationship



ORIX mascots should remain a separate visual family under the parent brand.



Do not incorporate mascots directly into the primary ORIX logo.



\### Brand architecture



\*\*ORIX\*\*

\- Parent brand.

\- Product identity.

\- Core application symbol.

\- Desktop interaction layer.



\*\*ORIX Mascots\*\*

\- Selectable personalities.

\- Character illustrations.

\- Expressive interactions.

\- Optional character packs.



\### Visual consistency



All mascots should share:

\- A recognizable design language.

\- Consistent rendering quality.

\- Distinctive silhouettes.

\- Expressive faces.

\- Cohesive proportions.

\- Consistent app-icon framing.



The mascot ecosystem can be colorful and expressive while the primary ORIX identity remains clean and monochromatic.

\## 10. Motion Identity



Create a simple motion language for the ORIX logo.



\### Proposed motion sequence



1\. The O-shaped boundary appears.

2\. The opening becomes visible.

3\. Two geometric paths converge.

4\. The X-inspired structure forms.

5\. The complete ORIX symbol settles into position.

6\. The ORIX wordmark appears.



\### Motion requirements



\- Smooth transitions.

\- Precise geometric movement.

\- Minimal animation duration.

\- No excessive bouncing.

\- No unnecessary glowing effects.

\- Consistent easing.

\- Suitable for application startup and onboarding.



Create static and animated versions.

\## 13. Quality Assurance \& Validation



Before finalizing the identity, evaluate:



\### Visual quality

\- Is the logo original?

\- Is the symbol recognizable without text?

\- Does the wordmark feel custom and balanced?

\- Is the geometry consistent?

\- Does the identity look premium?



\### Functional quality

\- Is it recognizable at 16×16?

\- Does it work as a system tray icon?

\- Does it work in Windows dark and light modes?

\- Does it scale without losing important details?

\- Does it work without gradients or shadows?



\### Brand quality

\- Does it represent ORIX rather than just the notch feature?

\- Can it expand into additional products and modules?

\- Does it coexist with the mascot ecosystem?

\- Is it distinctive enough to build a recognizable product identity?



Also perform trademark, domain, and existing-brand checks before committing to the final mark.

\## 15. Final Creative Principles



These are non-negotiable:



1\. Do not create a literal Mac Dynamic Island or notch icon.

2\. Do not use generic AI symbols, neural networks, or glowing orbs.

3\. Do not make mascots part of the primary logo.

4\. Do not depend on gradients to make the logo recognizable.

5\. Do not overcomplicate the geometry.

6\. Do not sacrifice small-size legibility.

7\. Do not use generic typography without customization.

8\. Do not create inconsistent logo variations.

9\. Do not finalize without testing real Windows applications.

10\. Do not compromise originality for visual trends.
